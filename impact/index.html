Housing Community: the impact of investors, lenders and givers

A $37,644,270 development at 365 Dunluce Road NW: 96 townhomes, 296 bedrooms, 72 units at below-market rent. The project is funded by grants, philanthropy, interest-free loans and investment. Each of those four sources is priced differently and repaid differently. This page shows what each one receives, and what the building produces, with the arithmetic for every figure.

Who the investors are

The raise is not addressed to the general market. It is addressed to four pools of capital that are already looking for exactly this: patient, Shariah-compliant, housing-focused, impact-measured. Each is large enough that Dunluce is a rounding error within it, which is the point. The constraint on this project has never been the amount of capital in the world. It is the number of investable, credible, faith-aligned housing deals.

$160B

Canadian philanthropic foundations

Over 11,000 public and private foundations hold roughly $160 billion in assets and granted $10.7 billion in 2023. A growing share are deploying endowment capital as impact and mission-related investment, not only as grants.

Philanthropic Foundations Canada, 2024 Landscape Report, T3010 2023 data

$755M

Federal Social Finance Fund

A repayable federal fund, with $400 million placed with three wholesalers (Boann Social Impact, Realize Capital Partners, Fonds de finance sociale CAP Finance) over the first five years. It is mandated to draw $2 of private capital for every public dollar, and at least 35% must go to initiatives advancing social equity.

Employment and Social Development Canada. As of December 2024 the wholesalers had produced over $250 million of investment in at least 83 social purpose organizations

$350M

Impact investing platforms

SVX clients have mobilised about $350 million in capital, with 1,200 investors engaged and more than 500 enterprises, organisations and funds supported. Its affordable housing and community real estate practice is directly relevant, including community bond structures for housing providers.

SVX, Our Impact page. Note that SVX's home page states $34.2 million on the same measure; the two pages disagree

1.8M

Canadian Muslim households

About 1.8 million Muslims in Canada as of the 2021 census, projected to reach 2.7 million by 2030. Only four providers offer Shariah-compliant home financing nationally, and there is no established vehicle for faith-aligned investment in affordable rental housing. This is the pool with the least competition for its capital and the strongest reason to prefer this structure.

The payout plan, period by period

The pro forma does not let surplus flow freely to redemption. It sets a fixed redemption tranche at the end of each period (Fund Revenue Share, rows 127 and 132), with equity bought out at the end of year 4 and refinanced through halal financing. Investors are repaid in full at the end of year 25. Where a tranche exceeds the operating surplus available in that period, the difference shows in the shortfall column: that is the gap philanthropy closes.

The equations behind the money

Modelled

Investor share of net operating income

Verified against the pro forma: period 2 gives 3.5% × $19,227,313 ÷ $1,025,845 = 65.6%, matching the locked value in the model. Every dollar of philanthropy or qard hasan reduces capital at work by one dollar and lowers this share.

Modelled

Charitable surplus per dollar given

Surplus is net operating income less the investor claim, less qard hasan repayment, less the redemption tranche due in that period. That surplus is what the pro forma makes available for charitable allocation, so it flows to IslamicFamily's programming: food security, housing support, family violence services. Baseline is the same building with $400,000 of philanthropy and nothing more.

Counted

People housed

Unit mix from the pro forma: 48 two-bedroom, 16 three-bedroom, 8 four-bedroom, 24 five-bedroom. 296 bedrooms total, 222 of them in the 72 below-market units.

Benchmarked

Grade 12 completion

Children are derived from the unit mix rather than assumed: 296 bedrooms at 1.5 people each is 444 residents across 96 homes, or 4.6 people per home, and subtracting two adults per household leaves 252 children resident at any time. Only children in the 72 below-market homes are counted for this effect, since theirs is the population with an inadequate-housing counterfactual. The benefit basis matters a great deal: crediting only children resident during their own Grade 12 year is the narrowest reading and crediting any full tenancy during school years the widest, a range of roughly three to one. The 13-point drop is IslamicFamily's own prior analysis of inadequate housing and Grade 12 completion. It is corroborated independently by Add Health, where adolescents with at least one move in a 12-month period showed roughly half the likelihood of holding a diploma by age 25, whether the move was to a poorer or a less-poor neighbourhood (PubMed 26188449). Converting that odds ratio against an 85% baseline yields an 11.1-point gap, close to the 13 used here. Both figures are observational associations, so treat the result as an order of magnitude.

Benchmarked

Lifetime earnings of children

$302,000 lifetime gain per child (about $99,000 in present value at age eight, discounted at 3%) is the Moving to Opportunity estimate for children who moved from high-poverty public housing to a lower-poverty neighbourhood before age 13 (Chetty, Hendren and Katz, American Economic Review 2016). Dunluce is a different intervention in a different country, so the transfer factor is a judgment, not a finding. At 100% transfer this figure would be indefensible; the default of 30% is deliberately conservative.

Why tenancy stability is the mechanism

Families in assisted housing show a 14% lower rate of residential moves than comparable families in private-market rentals.
Each additional housing quality problem predicts a 6% higher rate of moves.

Urban Institute Upward Mobility framework, drawing on Kull, Coley and Lynch. This is the link between the physical asset and the outcomes above: subsidised, well-maintained housing reduces moves, and reduced moves are what the education and earnings literature responds to.
