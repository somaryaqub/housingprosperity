Build-out of the fund

Pilot project

The Diamond at Dunluce, designed for families who need to stay together.

A 96-unit, 290-bedroom development at 365 Dunluce Road NW in Edmonton, purpose-built for larger and extended families, with occupancy expected in early 2028.

  • The bedroom count rose from 264 as the design developed.
  • Three quarters of the units are affordable, in three equal bands at 50%, 40% and 20% below market. The remaining quarter is at market rents.
  • A 40-year caveat sets the affordable share at no less than half of the units.
  • A community-based rent supplement model supports the deepest affordability.
  • Every grant dollar obtained reduces IslamicFamily's purchase price by the same amount.

Policy and government relations

Public money is already in the project. Next, the rules.

Our policy work aims to make federal capital, tax and program rules work for faith-rooted housing finance, and to bring the next level of government in.

Federal

  • Drafted a pre-budget submission on values-aligned housing finance, developed with the Islamic Finance Community of Practice.
  • Met Finance Canada in early July and followed up on four areas where clarity on federal capital, tax and program treatment would speed the work.
  • Submitted a Build Canada Homes application. The $4M request has moved to next steps.
  • Contributed to the federal briefing note on the financial exclusion of Muslims, and briefed a senior policy analyst at Employment and Social Development Canada.

Provincial

  • The Government of Alberta confirmed $9.3M through the Affordable Housing Partnership Program.
  • A 2024 meeting with the Assistant Deputy Minister of Housing opened the door to CMHC.
  • We wrote to the Minister of Assisted Living and Social Services on equity investment, received a reply, and invited him to the groundbreaking.
  • We are raising the project with the Premier's office.

Municipal and sector

  • The City of Edmonton confirmed $3.6M, and the Mayor and a City Councillor are invited to the groundbreaking.
  • We raised the project with community funders in the context of the City's four-year budget.
  • We are drafting a white paper, "Islamic finance isn't about mortgages. It never was.", for policymakers and economists.

Due diligence has surfaced policy gaps we are tracking for the next stage of advocacy, including the tax treatment of project income sponsored by a charity and withholding tax on repayments to a foreign lender.

Lessons and pivots

What Year 1 taught us.

  • People aren't ready to hear the word 'sharia' or 'halal'
    For many prospective investors fair finance, that is accessible by more Canadians, including Muslims, is seen as a niche issue.
  • Foundation calendars rarely match project deadlines.
    Decision cycles at foundations ran past our August timeline. We moved the first close to October 2026 and staged the capital: a back-up loan, and a deposit split into two instalments.
  • Philanthropy is the lever.
    Because every grant dollar reduces the purchase price, we reframed our donor message as return on philanthropy. Our model puts about $94 of total build behind each philanthropic dollar, including $9.00 from the City of Edmonton and $23.29 from the Government of Alberta. Federal and investor amounts are still pending.
  • Making Referrals and Opening Doors for Others has High Impact
    Laidlaw Foundation organized the Charitable Foundations of Canada session, and the University of Victoria put the work on the Victoria Forum stage.
  • Questions cluster around structure, returns and tax.
    Funders and lenders ask about the Shariah structure, returns, tax treatment and public commitments, which is why we built the Shariah primer and the investor FAQ.
