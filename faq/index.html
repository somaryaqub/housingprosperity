A.1What is the Housing Prosperity Fund?

The Housing Prosperity Fund is a Shariah-compliant affordable housing investment vehicle created to mobilise private and philanthropic capital into family-sized affordable housing in Canada. It is designed for projects with multi-bedroom units serving larger, extended and multi-generational families, the households least well served by the current housing market.

Its first project is the Diamond at Dunluce, a 96-unit development at 365 Dunluce Road NW in Edmonton, Alberta, built by Vivid Homes and to be owned and operated by IslamicFamily.

“Housing Prosperity Fund” is the programme name used in market-facing materials. The legal issuer is the Halal Affordable Housing Investment Fund Limited Partnership. Both names refer to the same vehicle, and the sponsor team is standardising on the Housing Prosperity Fund name for investor communications.

A.3Is this a single-project fund, or a platform for many projects?

The Housing Prosperity Fund LP is a single-project vehicle financing the Diamond at Dunluce. The Fund’s goal is to support multiple projects over time through subsequent LPs that each raise their own capital, so investors are never committed to future projects.

What carries across projects is the permanent General Partner joint venture, owned by IslamicFamily (60%) and SVX (40%) and governed by a JV agreement. That is the scaffolding to scale: shared management, governance, audit, and reporting, without pooling capital or risk across projects.

Investors subscribe as LP unitholders. The Fund provides the financing that enables IslamicFamily to acquire the property. IslamicFamily holds legal title; the Fund’s economic interest is secured by a Declaration of Trust and related security. IslamicFamily buys out the Fund over time. Part C sets out the mechanics.

On capital contributions specifically:

  • No forward commitment. Subscribing to this LP is a commitment only to this project. Nothing is ever called for a future project.
  • Each future project is capitalized through its own raise, in its own separate LP, with its own investors and its own security against its own asset.
  • An investor’s capital and security are tied to the project they actually back, and are not exposed to any other project in the pipeline.
  • Existing investors may be invited into a future LP, but that is a fresh, separate, entirely optional subscription.

B.5What is the evidence of demand?

There are approximately 12,222 Edmontonians waiting for affordable housing, with roughly 700 added every month. The wait is not evenly distributed: a one-bedroom unit carries roughly a one-year wait, while a three-bedroom or larger unit carries a wait of four years or more. The larger the family, the longer the wait.

That gap is structural rather than cyclical. Per-unit capital subsidy formulas reward smaller units, so a developer housing more people in fewer, larger units attracts less support per bedroom than one building small units. The Diamond at Dunluce is deliberately built against that grain, which is why 92% of its units have two or more bedrooms.

The operating consequence is the vacancy assumption discussed at E.5: comparable affordable family housing in Edmonton runs at zero vacancy with waiting lists in the thousands.

Transaction structure, ownership and investor security

Why the Fund never holds title, how public subsidy is treated in the price, what the Declaration of Trust gives investors, and where the Fund's charge ranks

C.1Why will the Fund never own the property? Why IslamicFamily?

This is a deliberate design choice, not a limitation.

The purpose of the Fund is to help a charity acquire and keep long-term ownership of community assets. IslamicFamily, as the charitable owner, is the permanent party; the Fund is the finite one. Investment capital enters, does its work, earns a defined return, and is bought out. What remains afterwards is a charity that owns a 96-unit affordable housing asset outright, with a forty-year affordability covenant on the land and the operating relationships to run it well.

If the Fund held the asset permanently, the outcome would be a private investment vehicle owning affordable housing, with the charity as a tenant or manager. The model is intended to do the opposite: allow purpose-driven investors to support charities and endowments in acquiring and maintaining long-term ownership and sovereignty over real estate and development projects. Consistent with that, the charity, rather than the investment fund, is the intended ultimate owner of the underlying property.

IslamicFamily is also the right operator. It is already connected to the tenant population through referrals and wraparound services, which is the operating advantage described at E.6.

This does not leave investors unsecured. The Fund holds a beneficial interest under a Declaration of Trust plus registered security, described immediately below.

C.2What is the Declaration of Trust, and what does it give investors?
Open item 4

A Declaration of Trust is a legal document that records the beneficial ownership of a property where the registered owner and the parties who contributed the money are different. The land registry shows who holds legal title. The Declaration of Trust shows who holds the beneficial interest, meaning who owns the financial value and receives the proceeds on a sale.

In this structure:

  • IslamicFamily holds legal title to the asset.
  • IslamicFamily grants a Declaration of Trust in favour of the General Partner on behalf of the Partnership, in respect of the amount funded or invested by the Limited Partners.
  • The beneficial interest recorded in the Declaration of Trust corresponds to the LP capital invested or advanced, net of any amounts repaid.
  • As the Fund is repaid and investors are redeemed, the beneficial interest decreases proportionally over time.
  • Legal title remains with IslamicFamily throughout the life of the arrangement.

The Declaration of Trust does two things at once. It avoids creating a new entity to hold the building, and it gives the Fund a recognised proprietary interest in the value of the asset rather than only a contractual claim. That proprietary connection matters for Shariah compliance: the investor must have a genuine ownership connection to the asset, not merely a right to be repaid.

One point of structuring detail is still being settled. For the arrangement to reflect true ownership under Shariah principles, the beneficial interest may need to be expressed as a percentage share rather than a fixed dollar amount, so that the Fund shares in changes in the value of the asset rather than holding a capped claim. That would require the property to be valued at each redemption stage, potentially by an index-based method rather than a full appraisal. This is with the Shariah advisor. See Part L, item 4.

C.3Walk us through the transaction
Open items 5, 7, 8, 20
  • Current ownership. Vivid Homes currently owns the land through an LP subsidiary. The City of Edmonton contributed the land, and the Province of Alberta has provided additional government funding.
  • Acquisition. IslamicFamily purchases the real estate. The Fund does not buy the real estate and will not be the registered owner at any point. The Fund advances the capital that enables IslamicFamily to complete the purchase.
  • Vendor financing. Vivid Homes provides vendor take-back financing for the balance of the purchase price. Vivid retains the project grants as collateral for four years and is paid out at the end of that period.
  • Possession. IslamicFamily takes possession and becomes the sole registered owner on title on occupancy closing. Occupancy is expected in early 2028.
  • Investor security. At closing, IslamicFamily grants the Declaration of Trust in favour of the General Partner on behalf of the Partnership, and the Fund registers its security against the property. The Fund’s charge ranks behind the vendor take-back financing until that financing is discharged.
  • Buyout. IslamicFamily progressively redeems the Fund’s interest. The beneficial interest under the Declaration of Trust reduces proportionally at each redemption stage until it is extinguished and IslamicFamily owns the asset free of the Fund’s interest.

The transaction is not a rent-to-own arrangement in the consumer sense. The rent-to-own language used in earlier materials described the economic effect, a charity acquiring an asset over time out of operating revenue, rather than the legal form. The legal form is an acquisition financed by the Fund and by vendor take-back financing, secured by a Declaration of Trust and a registered charge, and repaid through scheduled redemptions.

C.12How is the government support already in the project treated in the purchase price?

Part G

Legal, regulatory and subscription

Why there is no offering memorandum, who can invest, transfer restrictions, and tax treatment

G.1Why is there no offering memorandum or prospectus?

The Fund is relying on the private issuer exemption under Canadian securities law. A private issuer is not required to file a prospectus or deliver an offering memorandum, provided it stays within the conditions of the exemption. In outline, those conditions are:

  • the securities are subject to restrictions on transfer contained in the issuer’s constating documents or security holders’ agreements;
  • the issuer has no more than 50 holders of record, not counting employees and former employees, and counting joint holders as one; and
  • distributions are made only to the categories of purchaser permitted by the exemption, which include accredited investors, directors, officers and control persons of the issuer, and certain persons closely connected to them.

The practical consequences for investors are worth stating plainly. There is no offering memorandum, so the diligence record is the Limited Partnership Agreement, the Subscription Agreement, the Term Sheet, the business plan, the financial model and this document. There is no statutory right of action for misrepresentation of the kind that attaches to an offering memorandum. And the Fund cannot market the offering to the public, which is why materials are circulated to identified prospective investors rather than published.

Reliance on the exemption is a matter for counsel in each case, and the sponsor team confirms eligibility with counsel before accepting a subscription.

G.6What is the tax treatment for investors?
