Briefing Note for Honourable Shafqat Ali, President of the Treasury Board  |  August 3, 2026

Treasury's role in Supporting Equity-Deserving Communities to Build Affordable Housing

Issue

Two obstacles keep communities from building affordable housing and keep values-aligned investors from funding it, and both point to the Treasury Board:

  1. CMHC's senior leadership (President, CEO, and senior officials) are unsure whether they have the authority to decouple the agency's grant programs from its debt programs, preventing values-aligned communities from accessing the same grant programs as everyone else.
  2. Federal housing programs offer no established route to use equity, rather than debt, as a financing mechanism, which constrains the pool of capital available to build housing.

Background

A 4P partnership led by IslamicFamily (an Imagine Canada accredited charity), SVX (Canada's largest social impact fund developer) and Vivid Homes (a builder with 35+ years of experience) is building 264 new bedrooms (96 units) of affordable housing using the Housing Prosperity Fund, an innovative, scalable, values-aligned financing mechanism. The work is built on the award-winning, CMHC-funded Halal Housing Lab. The first project, the Diamond at Dunluce in Edmonton, is now under construction, with future projects lined up across Canada.

The barrier

CMHC contribution funding ordinarily requires taking on an accompanying interest-bearing loan. For a project financed by values-aligned capital, that single condition is disqualifying. It also blocks communities from directing new capital into affordable housing, such as ongoing community-based rent supplements that represent an estimated half a billion dollars in annual giving. This is a problem with program design, not statute: CMHC already runs a contribution-only viability assessment and already supports equity finance through its Affordable Housing Innovation Fund. Senior CMHC leadership advise the change needs confirmation and authorization at the Treasury Board level, which is why we are writing.

Considerations

  • No new appropriation. It changes how existing dollars flow, not how many, and draws in private capital that lessens reliance on government debt.
  • Better value for money. Removing this barrier will allow more communities to participate in building and maintaining housing, bringing new construction and operating capital to Canada’s largest crisis.
  • Timely and already committed. Budget 2024 pledged to expand access to alternative financing, including halal products; the Affordable Housing Fund is now folding into Build Canada Homes, whose terms are being set.

Requested action

The Board's role here is to enable, not to fund: to approve the terms and authorities under which a contribution, or an equity investment by a body such as CMHC or Build Canada Homes, can reach the fund. Any of the following helps, in order of preference; none needs new legislation or money.

  1. Enable a loan-optional (contribution-only) pathway, and a per-bedroom option for family-sized housing, within Build Canada Homes' terms and conditions as they are established.
  2. If the terms cannot be amended in time, support a one-time exception to program terms and conditions so a contribution can reach the Diamond at Dunluce.
  3. At minimum, direct Treasury Board Secretariat officials to work with CMHC, Build Canada Homes, and the Minister of Housing and Infrastructure to confirm the instrument and fastest lawful route, within a defined timeframe.
