A New Capital Model

A source of capital
no one else has unlocked.

حلال

Most affordable housing financing in Canada runs on interest-bearing debt, a structure that quietly locks out some of the most motivated capital in the country. Institutions and communities that collectively hold hundreds of millions in restricted, values-driven capital have been structurally excluded from building the housing they care about most.

This is also an invitation. Institutions locked out of Canada's debt-based housing system can do two things at once here: help build affordable housing today, and help fund an ongoing, community-based rent supplement, a source of operating capital that doesn't exist elsewhere in Canada.

Fund Mechanics

Structured for institutional rigour.
Designed for values.

For Impact Investors: investment shares are structured for high stability and predictable modest returns, backed by real assets and long-term operational revenue, and designed for institutional and accredited investors with a values-aligned mandate. Stable and Halal-compliant.

The Fund is structured as a GP/LP vehicle: General Partner jointly owned by IslamicFamily (60%) and SVX (40%). Investors subscribe as Limited Partners. Capital is deployed on an equity basis, with returns tied to real asset performance rather than interest or debt.

Risk, return and exit.

The Housing Prosperity Fund is built for patient, values-aligned capital. Here is what you can expect, what protects you, and what to weigh before you commit.

  • Stable, fixed-income-like returns A steady, predictable component of an impact portfolio, with a target return of approximately 3% a year for Class 1 investors.
  • Lower risk than debt-financed projects Returns come from a share of project revenue, not a fixed interest payment, so there is no interest obligation that can push the project into default.
  • Non-extractive and more representative The structure welcomes structurally excluded communities to build innovative solutions to Canadian challenges. Residual proceeds return to IslamicFamily for charitable purposes.
  • High-demand housing Under 4% expected vacancy and low turnover, in a city where larger families wait years for an affordable home.
  • 5 year suggested term, with flexibility Capital is returned at the end of the term, and flexible exits can be arranged.
Return: how you are paidTarget of approximately 3% a year, paid from rental revenue

Investor returns are tied to the performance of a real asset, not to interest. The target is a modest, steady return, designed for investors who value stability and impact over upside.

Source of return
A defined share of net operating income from rent. No interest is charged or paid, and the structure is designed to be Shariah-compliant.
Target return
Class 1: approximately 3% a year, above return of capital. Class 0: return of capital only, for mission-first and concessional investors.
When distributions begin
Shortly after occupancy, which is expected in early 2028. Distributions are paid annually, with the first targeted for mid-2028.
Order of payment
Investor distributions are paid before any residual flows to IslamicFamily. Losses are absorbed in reverse order, so the sponsor class takes loss before return-seeking investors. SVX does not share in residual profits beyond its 1.75% management fee.
What drives revenue
About $1.47 million of stabilized annual gross revenue, with roughly 19% left as cash surplus. Vacancy is modelled at 4% as a turnover allowance, while comparable affordable family housing in Edmonton runs close to zero vacancy.

Returns are targets, not guarantees. Distributions depend on the project's net operating income in each period.

Risk: what protects you, and what to weighNine risks, each with its protections

Exempt-market investments involve risk, including possible loss of capital. We set out the main risks plainly, alongside the protections built into the structure.

Construction and delivery A fixed-price construction contract caps cost overruns. The developer, Vivid Homes, self-finances construction. An independent owner's representative protects build quality. The building permit is in hand and construction is underway. Schedule delays would push back occupancy and the start of distributions.
Funding The City of Edmonton ($3.6M) and the Government of Alberta ($9.3M) are secured, along with the land. A federal Build Canada Homes request is not relied on. A dollar-for-dollar grant offset means every grant dollar lowers the purchase price. Affordability depth depends in part on grants that are not yet committed.
Security and ranking At closing, IslamicFamily grants the Fund a Declaration of Trust and a registered charge on the property. An expected property value of about $37 million sits against about $11 million of vendor financing ahead of the Fund. The Fund's charge is expected to rank behind the developer's vendor financing for about four years. Figures are approximate and subject to final documents.
Demand and occupancy More than 10,000 people are waiting for affordable housing in Edmonton, and larger families wait four years or more. 92% of units have two or more bedrooms. Revenue is modelled from comparable Edmonton affordable housing. Actual results may differ.
Operations A third-party property manager handles rent collection. IslamicFamily provides wraparound supports, financial literacy training and community-based rent supplements. No interest is charged on late rent. One asset, owned and operated by a charity.
Concentration and track record The Fund holds one project in its own limited partnership, with its own security. Investors have no commitment to future projects. The sponsors bring experience: SVX has mobilized more than $500M in impact capital, IslamicFamily has served communities for more than 40 years, and Vivid Homes has nearly 20 years of construction experience. No diversification, and this is the Fund's first project. The track record belongs to the parties behind it.
Governance and conflicts Limited partners can remove the General Partner by a two-thirds vote and receive annual audited statements and an impact report. An investor observer seat is planned on the General Partner board. The General Partner is owned 60% by IslamicFamily, which is also the counterparty to the Fund's principal transaction, and 40% by SVX, which manages the Fund.
Liquidity Capital returns through scheduled redemptions, with a suggested 5 year term and flexibility. Units are not freely tradeable and there is no secondary market.

Suggested term
5 years, with flexibility. Flexible exits can be arranged.
At the end of the term
Capital is returned.
How it works
IslamicFamily progressively buys out the Fund's ownership. As investors are redeemed, the Fund's beneficial ownership of the property shrinks until it is fully repaid.
What is left behind
A charity that owns 96 affordable homes outright, with a 40-year affordability covenant on the land.
Resale
Units are not freely tradeable. Return of capital comes through scheduled redemptions, not resale.
Measurement and reportingAnnual audited statements, SDGs and IRIS+ aligned metrics

Every investor receives annual audited financial statements and an annual impact report. We measure outcomes against recognized frameworks so you can report them in your own portfolio.

  • UN SDG 1: No poverty
  • UN SDG 4: Quality education
  • UN SDG 10: Reduced inequalities
  • UN SDG 11: Sustainable cities and communities
  • IRIS+ aligned metrics

Reporting covers affordability against the 40-year covenant, occupancy and household composition, the supports delivered through IslamicFamily, and progress on the outcomes in Impact in depth.

Target returns are not fixed, promised or guaranteed, and actual distributions depend on net operating income. Redemption timing is a plan, not a covenant. Exempt-market investments involve risk, including possible loss of capital. Nothing here is an offer to sell securities. Prospective investors should consult their own legal, tax and Shariah advisors.
